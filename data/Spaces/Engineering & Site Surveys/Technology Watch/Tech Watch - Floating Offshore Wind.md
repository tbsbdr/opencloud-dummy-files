# Tech Watch: Floating Offshore Wind

*Owner: Sofie Lindqvist · Status: living note · Last review July 2026*


## Why we care

Our offshore pipeline is fixed-bottom today (Harrow Bay: monopiles in 28–36 m). Several leasing rounds in the northern North Sea and around Norway now include sites in 60–200 m water depth, where only floating offshore wind works. We need an informed view before we bid.


## State of the market (mid-2026)

- Global installed floating capacity is still small (a few hundred MW), mostly pre-commercial arrays of 5–15 turbines.
- Three main concepts: semi-submersible (most common), spar, tension-leg platform (TLP).
- LCOE is roughly 1.8–2.5 × fixed-bottom; cost-down depends on serial fabrication and port capacity.
- Dynamic 66 kV array cables are qualified; dynamic export cables at 220 kV are not yet proven at scale.


## Key engineering questions for Weyland

- Port: is there a port with quayside for assembly of 15 MW turbines on floaters? Port of Eastholm has a 12 m draught – not enough for most semi-subs.
- Mooring and anchors in our typical soils (dense sand, stiff clay – see Harrow Bay ground model).
- Turbine compatibility: platform motion and controller tuning are turbine-specific; Halden has no floating track record yet.
- Wake losses behave differently with platform motion – current yield models are not validated for floating arrays.


## Recommendation

Do not bid as lead developer in floating rounds before 2028. Consider a minority partnership in a demonstration project to build know-how. Revisit in Q2 2027.
