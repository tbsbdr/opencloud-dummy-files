# Incoterms 2020 – quick guide for project buyers

We use **Incoterms 2020** in all purchase orders and contracts. Always write the rule *and* the named place,
e.g. `DAP Port of Eastholm, Quay 3`.

| Rule | Risk passes to us | Who books the main transport | Use at Weyland |
|---|---|---|---|
| EXW | At seller's premises | We do | Avoid – customs and loading are our problem |
| FCA | When handed to our carrier | We do | Small parts from EU suppliers |
| CPT / CIP | When handed to first carrier | Seller | Rare |
| DAP | On arrival, ready for unloading | Seller | **Default for major components** (turbines, monopiles) |
| DPU | After unloading at named place | Seller | When seller has the better crane / SPMT access |
| DDP | Delivered, duty paid | Seller | Only for UK domestic supplies |
| FOB / CIF | Ship's rail / on board | Seller (CIF) | Sea freight bulk – not for project cargo |

## Things that go wrong

- **"DAP Eastholm"** without quay → disputes about who moves the cargo between quays. Always name the quay.
- **Customs after Brexit:** for DK/DE → UK deliveries the importer of record must be agreed in the contract.
  For Harrow Bay it is the UK project company (EORI on file with Mateo).
- **Insurance:** under DAP the seller carries the transport risk, but our marine cargo policy still needs a notification
  for project cargo above GBP 5 m per voyage (Thames Insurance Brokers).
- Incoterms do **not** define when ownership passes – that is in the contract (usually on payment or delivery).
